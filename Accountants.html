WHAT WE HEAR

Start with the problem, not the tool.

Five things come up in nearly every conversation with a practice principal or a finance director. None of them is solved by buying another AI assistant.

01

Client data lives in a dozen systems

Ledger, practice management, payroll, document store, bank feeds, spreadsheets on a partner's desktop. Nobody can answer a portfolio-level question without a week of manual assembly.

02

Reconciliation is still people and spreadsheets

The month-end control that matters most is the one least automated — and the evidence of it lives in someone's inbox rather than in a system.

03

Nobody knows where client PII sits

TFNs, bank details and identity documents scattered across systems and archives, with retention obligations that are a policy document rather than a scheduled job.

04

The AI pilot can't touch client data

Risk says no, and risk is right — because there's no classification, no access model and no audit trail for what the model would see.

05

Technical knowledge is trapped in people

Rulings, policy notes, prior-year workpapers and internal precedent sit across SharePoint, email and a wiki. Juniors ask seniors; seniors become the bottleneck.

THE PATTERN

All five are data problems

Which is why we fix them in that order — consolidate, reconcile, classify, then automate. AI goes on last, once there's something underneath it worth asking questions of.
